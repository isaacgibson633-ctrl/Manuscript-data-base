How this list was made

Numbers

Manuscripts are named by their Gregory–Aland number, the standard catalogue kept by the Institute for New Testament Textual Research (INTF) in Münster. 𝔓 marks a papyrus; numbers starting with 0 are parchment majuscules (capital-letter manuscripts). Old Latin manuscripts also carry a Vetus Latina (VL) number.

Dates

For Greek manuscripts the headline date is the INTF catalogue date; for Latin manuscripts it follows the standard catalogues cited in each record. Where scholars have argued for other dates, the entry gives the range and names who proposed what. Almost all of these dates come from handwriting style (palaeography), not from carbon dating or a dated colophon.

Confidence labels

Firm: specialists broadly agree within about a century. Debated: published proposals differ by more than a century, or the catalogue date has been seriously challenged. The label is about the date, not the manuscript’s importance.

What “contains the verse” means

At least part of the passage survives on the page. For fragments, that can mean a handful of letters. Contents come from published catalogue descriptions and each manuscript’s recorded gaps (lacunae).

Photographs

Images stay with the libraries and projects that made them, so every entry links out rather than copying pictures. The INTF Virtual Manuscript Room link works for every Greek entry; other links go to the holding library or to CSNTM where a viewer exists.

Neutral language

Entries describe what a manuscript is, when it was likely made and how it reached its current home. They don’t argue for or against the reliability of the Gospels.
