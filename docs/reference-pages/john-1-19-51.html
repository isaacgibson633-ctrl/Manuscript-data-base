John 1:19–51: the Baptist and the first disciples

Four scenes: John the Baptist questioned by envoys from Jerusalem, his testimony about Jesus, the first disciples, and the call of Philip and Nathanael. For each verse: which Greek manuscripts up to AD 900 preserve it, where they disagree, and which early Latin manuscripts carry it.

Who preserves which verse

After the Prologue, the picture changes. Alongside the great codices, a cluster of small papyrus fragments from Oxyrhynchus (𝔓5, 𝔓106, 𝔓119, 𝔓120) and several parchment scraps cover these verses, so some verses have more early witnesses than the Prologue did. Codex Bezae’s Greek has a gap here, and Codex Ephraemi breaks off at 1:40.

Saved for the translation sweep

John 1:39 · translation question, not a manuscript variant

What time is “the tenth hour”?

ὥρα ἦν ὡς δεκάτηRead the same way in the early Greek manuscripts.

Counting hours from sunrise, as was usual in the ancient Mediterranean, the tenth hour is about 4 p.m., which is how several modern translations render it. A minority view holds that John counts from midnight, which would make it 10 a.m. The manuscripts do not settle this; it will be treated with other time references in John (4:6, 19:14) in the translation sweep.

The Latin layer

Latin evidence matters twice in these verses: three Old Latin manuscripts support “the Chosen One” at 1:34, and three support “hereafter” at 1:51.
